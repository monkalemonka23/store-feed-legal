# Store Feed: Help Center

Store Feed shows a "What's new" list on your store: new products, blog posts
and active promotions, collected automatically. This guide explains how to set
it up and what to do when something doesn't look right.

Each section has a number (for example **6.2**). If you contact support, you can
simply point to it.

### 1.2 Changing your plan

1. Open the Store Feed app in your Shopify admin.
2. Click **Plans** (or **Change plan**) in the top right corner.
3. Choose a plan and confirm it on the Shopify page.

When you move to a lower plan, Shopify switches it at the end of your current
billing period. Until then you keep the features you paid for.

### 1.3 Cancelling Starter or uninstalling

- **To stop paying and keep the app:** move to the Free plan (1.2). Shopify
  switches the plan at the end of the current billing period.
- **To remove the app:** uninstall it in **Settings > Apps and sales
  channels**. Shopify cancels the Starter subscription automatically, and the
  widget disappears from your store together with the app. You don't need to
  edit your theme.
- **Free trial:** Shopify counts the trial days used by your store, so
  uninstalling and installing again does not start a new 14-day trial.
- What happens to your data after uninstalling: see 9.3.
- **Refunds:** Starter follows Shopify's billing rules. Charges are billed
  in advance on your Shopify bill and are not refunded, also not for the
  unused part of a month or a year. Moving to Free or uninstalling stops
  the next charges. Use the 14-day free trial to try Starter before the
  first charge. Every Store Feed charge is listed in your Shopify admin,
  in **Settings > Billing**.

### 1.4 More than one store

Each store installs Store Feed separately and has its own plan and settings.
A Starter subscription covers the one store it was bought for.

## 2. Adding Store Feed to your store

### 2.1 The quick way

1. Open the Store Feed app.
2. In **Add Store Feed to your storefront**, click **Add** next to the page
   where you want the widget (Home page, Product pages, Collection pages, and
   so on).
3. The theme editor opens with Store Feed already added. Drag it where you want
   it and click **Save**.

Nothing changes on your store until you click **Save**: the theme editor is
your preview, with your own products and your own theme.

On product pages, **Add** puts Store Feed in the product section, under the
photos and product details. The best spot is in the column with **Add to
cart**: in the theme editor, drag Store Feed into that column, under the
button. Shoppers see the product first and your promotions right where they
decide to buy. A widget above the product photo pushes the photo down on
phones, which slows the page and gets in the shopper's way. If you place it
above the photo anyway (in the header or in a section before the product),
on product pages it starts folded to its title bar (4.5).

If your theme has more than one template for a page type (for example a second
product template), the app lists each template separately with its own **Add**
button. Products, collections or pages that use a given template show the
widget only when it is added to that template.

The app shows which pages already have the widget ("Added") and which copies
are hidden in the theme editor ("Hidden").

The widget belongs to the theme you added it to. After you publish another
theme, the app shows its pages as not added yet: click **Add** again.

Next to **Add Store Feed to your storefront** the app shows whether the widget
is live: **Active on your store**, or **Not on your store yet**.

### 2.2 Adding it yourself

1. Go to **Online Store > Themes > Customize**.
2. Open the page you want.
3. Click **Add section** and choose **Store Feed** under **Apps**.
4. Click **Save**.

If Store Feed is not on the list, your theme does not support app sections.
This is the case for some older themes.

### 2.3 Where it works best

- **In the header (recommended)**: it appears on every page, near the top,
  where shoppers look first. After a shopper clicks an entry, it folds on
  the next page, so it doesn't cover what they came to see (4.5).
  With a transparent header (the menu over the first image of the page, in
  themes such as Horizon or Ritual), the widget shows above the menu, so it
  never covers the image or the product title.
- **As its own section on a page**: good for the home page or a collection.
- **In the footer**: it appears on every page, but fewer shoppers scroll that far.
- **Not inside a product card or a product slider.** There it is repeated for
  every product in the grid.
- **It needs at least 200 px of width.** In very narrow spots (for example the
  row of icons in a header) it stays hidden.

**Page by page:**

- **Home page:** in the header, or as its own section near the top (below
  the main banner). New visitors see right away that the store is active:
  new arrivals, recent posts and running deals.
- **Product pages:** in the header, or as a section below the product
  details. A promotion that covers the product (or its collection) moves to
  the top and is marked **Viewing** (3.5), so the shopper sees the deal while
  deciding. A widget above the product photo starts folded, so the photo
  and the price stay in view (4.5).
- **Collection pages:** as a section above or below the product grid.
  Promotions on that collection are marked **Viewing** (3.5).
- **Blog posts:** as a section below the article. A reader who finishes
  the post sees new products and deals, a short way from reading to
  shopping. The post being read is skipped from the list (3.5).
- **Checkout:** not possible. Shopify doesn't allow app blocks on checkout
  pages, including the order status page. A code added with **Use code**
  is already in the cart when the shopper goes to checkout (3.3.3).

### 2.4 More than one place

You can add Store Feed in several places. Every copy shows the same list and
uses the same settings from the app.

**Recommended: 1-2 widgets per page, 3 at most**, for example one in the
header and one section on the home page. A header or footer widget counts on
every page. More copies on the same page show the same entries again and
make the page slower to load.

## 3. What appears in the feed

### 3.1 Products

A product appears when it is:

- **Active** (not a draft or archived),
- **available on the Online Store sales channel**,
- **published within the chosen time window** (last 7, 14 or 30 days).

Tip: a product added through an import or an app is sometimes active but not
available on the Online Store channel. Open the product in Shopify, check
**Sales channels** and make sure **Online Store** is ticked.

New products reach the widget with the next update: every night, or right
away with **Refresh now** (6.1, 6.2). The date that counts is the date the
product was published on the Online Store. A product scheduled to go live
later is not on the Online Store until that time, so it can join the feed
only with an update after it goes live.

Products that are sold out are not shown.

See also: 3.7 Choosing what to include (Starter)

### 3.2 Blog posts (Starter)

Starter only. Published blog posts from the chosen time window (last 7, 14
or 30 days). A post scheduled for later appears after it is published, with
the next update (6.1, 6.2).

See also: 3.7 Choosing what to include (Starter)

### 3.3 Promotions (Starter)

Starter only. Four parts: which promotions appear (3.3.1), what a
promotion card shows (3.3.2), the **Use code** and **Copy** buttons (3.3.3)
and tips for naming discounts in Shopify (3.3.4).

#### 3.3.1 Which promotions appear

- Promotions must be **active**.
- Promotions that **started before the chosen time window** still appear while
  they run, but only in places left free by everything else (3.4).
- **Automatic discounts** appear automatically.
- **Discount codes** appear when they are **available to all customers**, or
  when you add the tag `public` to a code that is limited to some customers.
  A tagged code is shown to everyone who sees the widget.
- Products that are sold out are not listed in a promotion. If nothing in a
  promotion can be bought any more, the promotion is not shown.
- Shopify Rollouts: only discounts that every shopper gets appear (3.8).

#### 3.3.2 What a promotion card shows

- Every card starts with a short hook: for promotions the discount value
  (for example "15% off" or "Get 1 item free") in bold, for products "New
  product", for blog posts "New post". The name is the second line. If a
  discount code has no name of its own in Shopify, the card shows only the
  value, and the code stays in the code box.
- Promotions show their end date, or **No end date** when they have none. They
  do not show a start date: shoppers care when a deal ends, not when it began.
- If a promotion has an end date, the widget shows the date and time it ends.
  Each shopper sees it in their own time zone and in the date format they are
  used to (for example "Nov 30, 5:59 PM" in New York or "30 Nov, 22:59" in
  London). In the last 6 days it also counts down ("Ends in 3 days"). When 3 days or fewer are
  left, the end date is shown in bold with a dot in your accent color.
- How the date format is chosen: first from the shopper's browser, if it
  has the store language with a region (for example "en-GB" gives
  "30 Nov, 22:59"). Only when the browser has no such setting, the widget
  uses the country selected in your store (Shopify Markets), for example
  Spanish with Mexico gives "30 nov, 11:59 p.m.". So changing the country in
  your store changes the format only for shoppers whose browser has no
  regional setting for the store language. Some combinations look the same:
  English with Mexico uses the same format as English with the United
  States.
- "Buy X, get Y" discounts show what to buy and what you get, with links.
  When both come from the same collection, the widget shows it once, as
  "Buy and get from: Bars".
- A discount on selected variants of a product (for example only some
  colors) lists the product with those variants, for example "The
  Complete Snowboard (Ice, Dawn)", and the link opens the product with
  the first of them selected.
- The widget shows the discount's terms: the amount, minimum purchase or
  quantity, one use per customer, the total number of uses, how many times it
  applies per order, and whether it can be combined with other discounts.
  The most useful terms come first (minimum purchase, where it applies,
  then usage limits and combinations). The discount value already shown in
  the hook is not repeated in the terms, and amounts drop empty cents
  ("$15 off", not "$15.00 off"). Terms that only repeat the collections or
  products listed on the card ("2 products") are left out, and general
  terms ("One-time purchase products", "For all countries") come last,
  after the discounts it can't be combined with.
  The first 2 lines are visible and filled; they end on a whole word
  (never in the middle of a word), so the card keeps its height when the
  page finishes loading. Collections and
  products show the first names that fit about one line (two lines when
  the card has no separate "Buy" and "Get"), the rest as "and N more".
  Each card has one
  "more", after the first part that is cut: it opens the terms and the
  collections or products together, and "less" at the very end closes
  them. Opened terms, collections and products are shown as a list, one
  per line. "more" has a small arrow and no underline: it opens text in place,
  while underlined names lead to another page.
- In the terms, a discount code lists the store's automatic discounts it
  can't be combined with, and says that the store applies the one that
  saves more.
- Automatic discounts (no code) show, where other cards have the code
  buttons, "Already applied in your cart" when the discount already
  applies in the shopper's cart, or "Applies automatically once you meet
  the terms above" when it doesn't yet, or "Not applied: ... gives a
  bigger discount for this cart" when another discount it can't be
  combined with applies instead. The text follows the cart when it
  changes, also when the shopper adds or removes products in the theme's
  cart.
  In the terms, an automatic discount lists the other automatic discounts
  and the discount codes from the widget it can't be combined with, the
  same way a code lists the automatic discounts. When it can't be combined
  with any code from the widget, it says so in one sentence instead of
  listing them all. When it combines with fewer codes than it doesn't, it
  names only the codes it combines with (for example "Combines only with
  discount code SHIPUS3").
- Codes for selected customers only (a customer segment or a list of
  customers, shown because of the "public" tag) say "For selected customers
  only" in their terms. Shopify checks at checkout whether the shopper
  qualifies, so after **Use code** the shopper is told that the code is for
  selected customers and that checkout shows whether it applies.

#### 3.3.3 Use code and Copy buttons

Discount codes have two buttons. Automatic discounts have none: they show
a status line instead (3.3.2).

- **Use code** adds the code to the shopper's cart, so they don't have to
  type it at checkout. Codes already in the cart stay there. After the
  code is added, the button changes to **View cart** and opens the cart
  (the theme's cart drawer, or the cart page when the theme has none).
  Once the cart has been opened (with this button or from the theme) and
  has products in it, the button changes to **Check out** and takes the
  shopper to checkout. If the cart has been emptied in the meantime, the
  button goes back to **View cart** and opens the cart.
  The button keeps the same size for every label, so nothing on the card
  moves when it changes. The widget
  uses Shopify's built-in cart action, so an open cart drawer or cart
  section of the theme shows the new discount right away (themes Shopify
  doesn't recognize reload the page; the message still shows). Shopify decides
  which codes apply, and the widget tells the shopper the result:
  - the code applies: "Code added to your cart.", plus which other codes
    still apply and which codes or automatic discounts stopped applying
    because they can't be combined with it;
  - another discount in the cart already applies and, by the settings of
    both discounts, can't be combined with this one: the shopper sees
    which one it is and that it gives the bigger discount (Shopify keeps
    the discount that saves more when two can't be combined);
  - 5 product or order codes already apply (Shopify's limit per order)
    and the new code can be combined with all of them: the widget
    doesn't add it, the button stays **Use code**, and the shopper is asked
    to remove one first. Codes can be removed at checkout;
  - shipping discount codes: the shipping discount shows at checkout,
    after the address is known. A code only for other countries than the
    shopper's market says which countries it is for;
  - a code for specific products when none of them is in the cart: the
    shopper is told that the code applies only to the products listed
    on the card, and the button changes to
    **Shop now**, which opens the page the card leads to;
  - the cart doesn't meet the discount's terms yet (for example it's
    empty): the code waits in the cart and applies once the cart meets
    them; the button changes to **Shop now**. For "buy X, get Y" codes
    there is no button, only the message, because the widget can't tell
    whether X or Y is missing.
  If the cart doesn't return the code or its end date has passed, the
  shopper is told the code isn't active anymore. If adding fails, the
  shopper is asked to use Copy instead. A code that is already in the
  cart shows **View cart** when the page loads (or **Shop now**, or no
  button, while it waits for the cart to meet its terms), and at the end
  of its terms (under "more", so nothing on the page moves) "This code is
  already in your cart.", plus the reason when it doesn't apply (another discount it
  can't be combined with, or a country it isn't for). When the shopper
  changes the products in the theme's cart, the widget reads the cart
  again and updates the buttons and these notes.
- **Copy** copies the code, for shoppers who prefer to paste it at checkout.
Codes that contain a comma show only Copy (Shopify's cart can't take them
from a button). The code is never shortened. The code and its buttons
start under the card's icon and use the card's full width, so the card
looks the same before and after the page finishes loading. When the
code and the buttons still don't fit side by side, the code goes above
the buttons, and **Use code** and **Copy** stay together.

#### 3.3.4 Naming discounts in Shopify

The widget shows your discount names on cards. Tips:

- The widget shows the discount value ("2% off") on its own line above the
  name. Let the name say why or for whom, not how much: "Review Reward",
  "Early Holiday Access", "Welcome Savings" read better than "Review
  Reward: 2% off" or "15% off Bars and Bites", which repeat the value.
- Leave thresholds and conditions ("over $50", "3+ items") out of the
  name too: the widget shows them from the discount's settings in the
  terms under the name.
- Short codes are easier to read and to type when copying fails: "SWEET2"
  works better than "EARLYACCESS15". The code is never shortened on the
  card.

### 3.4 When there are more updates than places

The number of entries is set in **Max entries**: 6, 12 or 24 (Free: 6).
These numbers fill whole rows in both the 2- and 3-column layout. The
widget uses 2 columns from about 600 px of width and 3 from about 900 px.

1. Promotions are picked first, then blog posts, then products.
2. Within each kind, the newest are picked.
3. Promotions that started before the time window come last: they fill only
   the places that are still free.
4. The chosen entries are then shown **newest first**, read across the
   rows: the newest is first on the left, the next one to its right, and
   so on, row by row. Cards can have different heights; when the columns
   drift apart by more than about 60 px, the next row starts level again,
   leaving a small gap under the shorter cards, so the rows stay easy to
   read.
5. A promotion that already applies in the shopper's cart (an automatic
   discount, or a code they added) is shown at the end of the list: the
   shopper already has it, so newer offers come first.

Example: 2 promotions, 3 blog posts and 6 new products, with 6 places, the
widget shows the 2 promotions, the 3 blog posts and the newest product,
sorted by date.

### 3.5 On the page of a listed item

When a shopper is already on the page of a product or blog post from the list,
that entry is skipped and the next one takes its place. Promotions stay, move
to the top of the list (unless they already apply in the cart, see 3.4) and
are marked **Viewing**, under the discount value. For a "buy X, get Y"
promotion seen on the page of what the shopper gets (not of what they need
to buy), the badge says **Reward from this page**. On a product page the
same goes for promotions on a collection the product belongs to. A
discount on the whole store or on the order (no products or collections
of its own) is never marked.

A promotion card leads to the page where the shopper finds what it applies
to: its collection (the biggest one, when there are several), its product,
or the first of its products when there are several. A discount on the
whole store or on the order leads to all products, newest first.

### 3.6 Nothing new to show

If there is nothing new in the chosen time window and no promotion is running,
the widget stays hidden. You can choose a longer window (30 days) in
**Date range**.

### 3.7 Choosing what to include (Starter)

Starter only. On the Free plan, all new products can appear.

- **Collections**: show only products from the chosen collections.
- **Blogs**: show only posts from the chosen blogs.
- **Exclude products with tag**: products with any of these tags never
  appear (useful for items that are always on sale and are not really "new").

Your saved choices are shown as soon as the app opens. A collection or blog
you have since deleted from your store is marked **Removed from store**, so
you can untick it.

### 3.8 Discounts in Shopify Rollouts (Starter)

Starter only (promotions are part of Starter, 3.3).

A Shopify **Rollout** can turn a discount on or off, and it can do that for
only a share of your traffic as a test.

Store Feed promotes only discounts that **every** shopper gets. A discount
that a rollout gives to part of your traffic stays out of the widget until it
reaches everyone. A discount that a rollout turns on for all traffic appears
in the widget.

You do not need to refresh anything: the widget updates on its own when a
rollout starts, changes or finishes.

## 4. Appearance: look and feel

### 4.1 Widget title

Shown at the top of the widget. You can use an emoji.

Ideas: a short title that says there is something new, in your store's
voice. Keep it to 2-4 words, so it fits on one line on a phone.

- General: "What's New", "New in store", "Fresh this week", "Just in"
- Deals first: "New & on sale", "Latest drops and deals"
- With an emoji: "✨ What's New", "🔥 Hot right now", "🎁 New for you"
- Seasonal: "New for autumn", "Holiday picks", "Back to school"

### 4.2 Accent color (Starter)

Starter only. On the Free plan these details use the text color of the
section the widget sits in.

Used for the stripe and the discount value on promotion cards, the
**Use code** button, the entry count, "more" and small details. Text on the
button and the count switches between black and white automatically, so it
stays readable. Where the accent color would be hard to read on your
background, the widget uses your text color instead.

Enter the color as a hex code in the `#RRGGBB` format, for example `#7F77DD`.

### 4.3 Icon style (Starter)

Starter only: Flat, 3D or Emoticon. The Free plan uses Emoticon.

### 4.3a Product and blog images (Starter)

Starter only. On the Free plan, products show an icon.

**Show product and blog images** (on by default): products and blog
posts show their main image (64 x 64 px) instead of an icon. Entries
without an image, and promotions, keep the icon.

### 4.3b Language of the widget

Labels in the widget (Copy, Promo, Ends in..., dates, Viewing) follow your
store's language: English, Polish, German, French, Spanish (Latin America),
Italian, Dutch and Portuguese (Brazil). Other languages use English. Dates
and times follow the usual format of each language, for example
"18 oct 2026, 6:05 p.m." in Spanish. The widget title is your own text.
Discount terms come from Shopify and are shown in English.

### 4.4 Corner style

Sharp, Rounded or Pill.

### 4.5 How the widget opens and its height (Starter)

Choosing **Open** or **Collapsed** and the height is Starter only. On the
Free plan the widget is set to Open with the default height. The rules in
"When the widget starts folded" below work on every plan.

- **Open (recommended)**: shoppers see the list straight away. On product
  pages, a widget above the product photo starts folded (rule 4 below).
- **Collapsed**: only the title bar; shoppers open it with one tap.
- **Height when open**: how much of the screen the open widget may take. When
  the list is longer, shoppers can scroll it inside the widget, or tap
  **Show all** below it to open the whole list. **Show less** brings it back
  to this height. Opening the list, the terms ("more" / "less") or the
  whole widget keeps the entry the shopper is reading in the same place on
  the screen; **Show less** puts that entry at the top of the shorter list.

**When the widget starts folded (every plan).** The first rule that fits
decides:

1. The shopper closed it with the close button: it stays hidden until they
   close the browser tab (4.6).
2. The shopper has just clicked an entry: on the page they land on, a widget
   in the top half of the screen starts folded to its title bar, so the list
   doesn't cover the product or post they came to see.
3. The shopper opened or folded this copy of the widget earlier in the
   visit: it stays the way they left it while they browse your store.
4. On product pages, a widget above the product photo (in the header or in
   a section before the product) starts folded, so the photo stays on the
   first screen (2.1).
5. Otherwise your setting decides: Open or Collapsed.

So a widget set to **Open** can start folded because of rules 2 to 4. One
tap on the title bar opens it.

### 4.6 Close button

Lets shoppers hide the widget. It stays hidden for them until they close the
browser tab.

### 4.7 Visibility by device

Show or hide the widget separately on desktop, tablet and mobile.

### 4.8 Footer

- **Free plan:** a small Store Feed icon at the bottom of the widget.
- **Starter:** no footer, or your own text (for example "Free shipping over
  $50"), optionally with a link. If the text is empty, nothing is shown.

Ideas for the Starter footer: one short line that gives one more reason to
buy or to come back, with a link where it helps.

- Shipping: "Free shipping over $50", "Same-day dispatch before 2 pm"
- Returns and trust: "30-day free returns", "Handmade in small batches"
- Coming back: "New arrivals every Friday", "Get 10% off: join our newsletter"
  (link to the sign-up page)
- Help: "Questions? Write to us" (link to your contact page)

### 4.9 How it matches your theme

The widget takes its look from your theme when the page loads, with no
settings to fill in:

- **Fonts:** the widget title uses the font, weight and color of your
  theme's section headings; entry titles use those of your product card
  titles; the rest uses your theme's text font.
- **Sizes:** text follows your theme's text size (between 12 and 18 px) and
  line spacing. Entry titles are at least 16 px and never smaller than the
  text. The widget title follows your headings (16-26 px) and is always a
  little larger than the entry titles.
- **Width:** never wider than your theme's content area, centered, with the
  same spacing above and below as your theme's sections (at least 16 px).
  The list uses 2 columns from about 600 px of width and 3 from about
  900 px (3.4).
- **Colors:** from the section the widget sits in (5.1). The border shown
  when a shopper points at an entry uses your theme's button or card hover
  color, or the accent color (4.2).
- **Corners:** set in **Corner style** (4.4).

## 5. Colors and background

### 5.1 Where the colors come from

You don't set a background for the widget. It takes the **background and text
colors of the section it sits in**, so it looks like part of your theme.

### 5.2 Changing the widget background

Change the colors of the section that holds the widget:

1. Go to **Online Store > Themes > Customize**.
2. Click the section where Store Feed is placed.
3. Change its **Color scheme** to one with the background you want.
4. Click **Save**.

The widget follows the new colors. If the text would be hard to read, it
switches to black or white text color on its own.

### 5.3 Section with a background image

If the section uses an image as its background, the widget uses the section's
**color scheme** background as a solid color, so the list stays readable. To
change it, change the section's color scheme (see 5.2).

### 5.4 Page with a gradient or image background

If the whole page has a gradient or image background, the widget stays
see-through so the background shows behind it.

## 6. Updates and changes

### 6.1 Automatic update

The list updates on its own every night at 00:15 UTC (for example 7:15 or
8:15 pm the evening before in New York, 1:15 or 2:15 am in Paris, depending
on daylight saving time). The app shows this time in your store's time zone,
under **Refresh now**. When you create, change, turn off or delete a
discount in Shopify, or a Shopify Rollout
changes, the widget updates on its own a few seconds later. New products
and blog posts wait for the nightly update, or for **Refresh now** (6.2).

### 6.2 Updating now

Click **Refresh now** in the app. You can do this once every 5 minutes. If it
was clicked recently, the button shows the time when it becomes available
again.

### 6.3 After changing settings

1. Click **Save**, either in the preview column or on the bar Shopify shows
   at the top when you have unsaved changes. The preview in the app now shows
   your real entries with the new settings (how many, from which dates,
   collections and blogs).
2. Saved settings reach your store with the nightly update. To send them
   now, click **Refresh now**.
3. Reload your store page (Ctrl+F5 on Windows, Cmd+Shift+R on Mac).

Shoppers see the change on the next page they open.

### 6.4 After changing your plan

Open the Store Feed app once after the change. The widget then updates to the
new plan by itself.

## 7. Analytics: Statistics (Starter)

Starter only.

What the numbers in the app mean:

- **Views** ("pages where shoppers saw the widget"): on how many pages
  shoppers saw the widget on their screen.
- **Clicks** ("clicks on entries and Use code"): how many times shoppers
  clicked an entry, a collection or product name on a promotion card, or
  **Use code**. **Copy** is not counted.
- **CTR** ("clicks / views"): clicks divided by views. For example, 5%
  means 5 clicks for every 100 views.
- **By device**: the same numbers for desktop, tablet and mobile, for the
  last 7, 30 or 90 days.
- **By widget setup**: each copy of the widget on its own. **Widget title**
  is the title it showed, **Started** says whether it started open or
  folded to its title bar, **Entries** how many entries it showed. Change
  the title or the number of entries and compare the CTR before and after.

How they are counted:

- **A view counts when the widget appears on the shopper's screen** (at
  least its title bar), not when the page loads. A widget lower on the page
  counts only once the shopper scrolls to it.
- **One view per page**, however many copies of the widget the page shows.
  A page with the widget in the header and another on the home page counts
  as one view, not two. A widget folded to its title bar counts too.
- **Every click counts once**, in whichever copy it happens: an entry, a
  collection or product name on a promotion card, or **Use code**.
  **Copy** is not counted.
- Views, clicks and click rate at the top count the whole store together.
  **By widget setup** counts each copy separately, so a page with two
  copies adds a view to each, and its views can add up to more than the
  total.

The widget sends its counts through your store's own address (the Shopify app
proxy), and Shopify signs every request, so no one can add numbers to another
store's statistics. Bots, crawlers and automated browsers are not counted.
The widget counts a shopper only when your store allows analytics for them
(Shopify's customer privacy settings and cookie banner): shoppers who
decline analytics are not counted at all.

Statistics are kept for 180 days; the app shows the last 90. Older days are
deleted every night. After you uninstall, all statistics are deleted with
the rest of your store's data (9.3).

If statistics stop updating: if you changed the app proxy address in
**Settings > Apps and sales channels > Store Feed > App proxy > Customize URL**,
change it back to `/apps/store-feed`. The widget sends its counts to that
address.

## 8. FAQ: Something doesn't look right

### 8.1 I can't see the widget on my store

1. Look at the badge next to **Add Store Feed to your storefront**. If it
   reads **Not on your store yet**, the widget has not been placed in your
   theme (2.1).
2. Check that it is added to that page and not hidden (the app shows "Added"
   next to the page, not "Hidden", see 2.1).
3. Check **Visibility by device** (4.7) for the device you are using.
4. Check that there is something new in the time window (3.6).
5. If you clicked the close button, it stays hidden until you close the
   browser tab (4.6).
6. If it sits in a very narrow spot, move it to its own section (2.3).

### 8.2 A new product is missing

1. The product is **Active** and available on **Online Store** (3.1).
2. It was published within the time window (3.1).
3. It doesn't have an excluded tag and belongs to the chosen collections (3.7).
4. There are enough places. Promotions and blog posts are picked first (3.4).
5. Click **Refresh now** (6.2).

### 8.3 A discount code is missing (Starter)

Promotions and discount codes appear only on the Starter plan (1.1).

1. The discount is active. If it started before the time window, it shows only
   when there are free places left (3.4).
2. It is available to all customers, or it has the tag `public` (3.3.1).
3. At least one of its products can still be bought (3.3.1).
4. If a Shopify Rollout gives it to only part of your traffic, it waits until
   the rollout reaches everyone (3.8).
5. Click **Refresh now** (6.2).

### 8.4 The background color doesn't match

The widget uses the colors of its section. Change the section's color scheme
(See: 5.2).

### 8.5 I changed the look and I don't see the changes

1. In the app, click **Save**.
2. Click **Refresh now**. If the button is locked, wait until the time shown
   on it (6.2).
3. Wait for the green message **"Feed refreshed: N entries in your widget"**.
   Only then are your changes sent to your store. If you see a yellow message
   instead, try again in a few minutes.
4. Open your store page and reload it without the saved copy:
   - **Windows** (Chrome, Edge, Firefox): press **Ctrl + F5**
     (or **Ctrl + Shift + R**).
   - **Mac** (Chrome, Edge, Firefox): press **Cmd + Shift + R**.
   - **Mac** (Safari): press **Cmd + Option + R**.
5. Still the old look? Open your store in a private window:
   - Chrome and Edge: **Ctrl + Shift + N** (Windows), **Cmd + Shift + N** (Mac).
   - Firefox: **Ctrl + Shift + P** (Windows), **Cmd + Shift + P** (Mac).
   - Safari: **Cmd + Shift + N**.

Shoppers see the change on the next page they open.

### 8.6 The widget repeats many times

It was added inside a product card or slider. Remove it there and add it as its
own section (2.3).

### 8.7 Store Feed is not on the list of sections

Your theme does not support app sections (2.2).

## 9. Privacy, speed, accessibility and search engines

### 9.1 What the app reads and stores

Store Feed reads your products, blog articles, discounts and discount
rollouts, and which sections of your theme carry the widget. It requests no
customer permissions and stores no personal data about your shoppers. It
stores your store's address, the access Shopify gives the app, your widget
settings, the current feed, daily totals of views and clicks per device
type, and on Starter the same totals per widget title, starting state
(open or folded) and number of entries (7). Details: the privacy policy on growboost.pl.

### 9.2 Cookies

The widget sets no cookies. It uses the browser's session storage to
remember, for that shopper only: that they closed or folded the widget,
that they have just clicked an entry (4.5), and a code they are adding to
the cart. Nothing of this is sent to our servers, and the browser clears it
when the session ends.

The widget's view and click counts follow your store's customer privacy
settings: they are sent only when the shopper allows analytics (7).

### 9.3 Your data after you uninstall

When you uninstall the app, we mark your store as uninstalled and stop
reading your store's data. About 48 hours later Shopify asks apps to erase a
store's data, and we then permanently delete everything stored for your
store: settings, the feed and statistics. If Shopify's request does not
arrive, we delete it ourselves 20 days after you uninstall. The widget
disappears from your store together with the app (1.3).

### 9.4 Page speed and layout shift

- The widget comes built into your store's pages, so nothing has to be
  fetched to show it after the page loads.
- Its script starts only when the shopper first moves, taps, scrolls or
  types, or about 10 seconds after the page has loaded.
- The widget's place on the page is set before the page is shown, so the
  page does not jump when the widget appears.
- Every copy of the widget adds to the page. Use 1-2 per page, 3 at most
  (2.4).

### 9.5 Accessibility

The widget is built against WCAG 2.1 AA:

- Every button and link has a name for screen readers that includes the
  words you see on it; **Use code** and **Copy** also name the code they
  act on.
- Screen readers announce the result of **Use code** and **Copy**.
- Everything works with the keyboard, with a visible focus outline.
- On touch screens, "more", "less" and the names in lists have tap areas
  of about 44 px.
- Text color switches to black or white when the section's colors would
  make it hard to read (5.2).
- Titles are never smaller than 16 px, and descriptions, dates, labels and
  discount terms never smaller than 13 px, whatever the theme uses.
- With "reduce motion" set on the device, the small repeating hint on the
  folded widget's arrow stops.

### 9.6 Search engines

The widget is part of your page's HTML, so its entries are ordinary links
that search engines can follow. It also adds a short list of the entries in
structured data (schema.org ItemList).

## 10. What Store Feed does not do

- It does not send emails, text messages or push notifications.
- It does not create, change or end discounts, and it does not change
  prices. It shows the discounts you set up in Shopify.
- It does not show prices or stock levels on its cards.
- It does not appear in checkout. Shopify's checkout does not show theme
  sections.
- It does not run A/B tests and does not show different lists to different
  shoppers. Every shopper sees the same list; only the notes about their
  own cart and the **Viewing** mark (3.5) differ.
- It does not show drafts, archived or unpublished products, sold-out
  products, or discount codes limited to some customers (unless you tag
  them `public`, 3.3.1).
- It does not translate your product names, post titles or discount terms
  (4.3b). Names and titles are shown in your store's default language, also
  when you translated them with Translate & Adapt, and the widget title is
  one text for all languages.
- It does not adjust the list to Shopify Markets or B2B catalogs: shoppers
  in every market see the same entries.
- It does not work on headless storefronts (Hydrogen or your own
  front end): the widget is a block for Online Store themes.
- It does not change your theme's code. Removing the block, or the app,
  removes the widget (1.3).
- It does not collect data about your shoppers and does not use cookies
  (9.1, 9.2).
- Its statistics count views and clicks only, not orders or revenue (7).

## 11. Before you install: common questions

### 11.1 A large store with heavy traffic

For example 100,000 products and millions of visits a month:

- The widget is part of your store's pages, and Shopify serves it with them.
  Our servers are not involved in showing it, so heavy traffic does not
  slow it down, and the widget keeps working even if our servers are
  briefly unavailable.
- The size of your catalog does not matter. Each update reads only the
  newest products from the time window (up to 50, or up to 50 per chosen
  collection), the newest blog posts and your active discounts.
- Each page with the widget sends at most one small statistics request
  (counts of views and clicks), when the shopper leaves the page or after
  30 seconds. If our server is busy, some counts can be lost; the widget
  itself is not affected.
- Page speed: see 9.4, and use 1-2 widgets per page, 3 at most (2.4).

### 11.2 A small store with little traffic

For example 20 products and 5,000 visits a month:

- We can't promise more sales. No app can, for every store.
- What the widget does: it shows shoppers what is new and which
  promotions are running, on every page where you place it, and each entry
  links straight to the product, post or collection. Discount codes can be
  added to the cart with one tap (3.3.3).
- With few new products, the list can be short or empty. Choose the
  30-day window (3.6), and add blog posts and promotions (Starter) to keep
  it full.
- Try it for free: the Free plan has no time limit, and Starter starts
  with a 14-day free trial (1.1). With Starter, the statistics (7) show
  how many shoppers click the entries.

### 11.3 The worst that can happen

- **Slower pages.** Every copy of the widget adds to the page. Use 1-2 per
  page, 3 at most (2.4, 9.4).
- **Out-of-date entries for a while.** New products and blog posts reach
  the widget with the nightly update, or with **Refresh now** (6.1, 6.2).
  Discounts update within seconds.
- **A code that some shoppers can't use.** A code you tag `public` is shown
  to everyone, but Shopify still checks at checkout who may use it (3.3.2).
- **Missing statistics.** If counts can't be sent, some views and clicks
  are not counted (11.1).

It can't change your prices, discounts or checkout, and it doesn't change
your theme's code (10). Removing the block, or uninstalling the app,
removes the widget (1.3).

### 11.4 Which themes is it tested on?

We test Store Feed on these Shopify themes: Horizon, Dawn, Savor, Craft,
Ritual, Publisher, Heritage and Taste. It works with any Online Store 2.0
theme that supports app blocks (2.2) and takes its look from your theme
(4.9), so a theme that is not on this list, or one you have customized,
will most likely work too. If something doesn't look right on your theme,
write to us (12) with the theme name, the page address and a screenshot.
We look at every report, and a fix in the app reaches every store.
